# Milestone 1: Pick a community and read it

## Community

**Community:** [r/running](https://www.reddit.com/r/running/)

I chose this public Reddit community because it has a large stream of
full-text posts with useful variation: training logs, race reports, questions,
gear discussions, injury and recovery experiences, and opinionated advice. It
is not tied to one show or release, so a reader does not need spoiler
homework. I read 36 recent public posts on October 14, 2026, using the
community page and public search results.

## Candidate distinctions from the reading

1. **Evidence-backed analysis vs. unsupported take:** Some posts cite splits,
   mileage, heart rate, dates, or comparisons. Others make a strong claim
   without checkable evidence.
2. **Personal report vs. general advice:** Some posts describe what happened
   to the author. Others tell other runners what they should do.
3. **Question/request vs. conclusion:** Some posts mainly ask for help or
   recommendations. Others mainly present a conclusion, even when they invite
   replies.
4. **Training/race performance vs. gear/logistics:** Some posts focus on
   fitness, pacing, or race outcomes. Others focus on shoes, watches, fees,
   routes, or event organization.

The first distinction looks like the most promising label boundary because
the community mixes careful mini-analyses with confident hot takes. I will
test that boundary against more posts before finalizing the taxonomy.

## Reading notes

The hardest cases were posts that included one personal number and then made a
general recommendation, or questions that contained a detailed analysis
before the question at the end. Those cases are exactly why the labels need a
written decision rule before the 200-row dataset is built. Future me gets a
rule; future me does not get to argue with past me.
